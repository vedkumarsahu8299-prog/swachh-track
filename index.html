Waste doesn't need to wait for someone to notice it.

Swachh Track lets any resident photograph and pin uncollected waste in their area. The report reaches the nearest municipal vehicle instantly, and the map shows the fix happening — not just the complaint being filed.

How a report becomes a collection

01 — Process

Sign in

Residents register once with their name, phone number and ward.

Photograph the waste

A photo is taken where the waste sits — no description required.

Mark the location

Location is captured automatically and can be fine-tuned on the map.

Vehicle is notified

The nearest municipal vehicle sees the pin, with the photo attached.

Collected & rewarded

Once cleared, the report closes. Reporters earn points; recyclables earn cash.

Where this could still go wrong

03 — Feasibility & viability

User adoption & engagement

Residents won't keep reporting into a system that feels ignored.

Mitigation: first-use training and a visible feedback loop.

Sustaining interest

Novelty fades once the first few reports are filed.

Mitigation: regular updates and a running rewards balance.

Technical integration

The app must work on the phones people already own.

Mitigation: keep it lightweight for low-end Android devices.

Municipality collaboration

None of this matters if the vehicle side never adopts it.

Mitigation: pilot with one ward's drivers first.

Who benefits, and how

04 — Impact & benefits

Environment

Faster, targeted collection means less waste sits long enough to be burned, dumped, or washed into drains.

Regulatory compliance

Timestamped collection records give municipalities a paper trail against disposal standards.

Public health

Clearing waste sooner cuts down breeding ground for vector-borne disease.

Individual residents

Every report has a visible outcome, and every recyclable handed in has a payout.
